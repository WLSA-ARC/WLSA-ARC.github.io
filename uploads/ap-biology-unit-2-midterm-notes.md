# AP Biology Unit 2 Review Notes

## Cell Theory

1. Cells are the fundamental units of life.

2. All organisms are composed of cells.

3. All cells come from pre‑existing cells.

## Surface Area‑to‑Volume Ratio

- As cell size increases, volume increases faster than surface area → SA/V ratio decreases.

- Higher surface area → higher substance exchange rate.

- Larger volume → higher consumption & secretion demand → lower exchange rate.

Conclusion: Small cell has larger SA/V ratio → more efficient material exchange.

## Two Major Cell Types

- Prokaryotes: no membrane‑enclosed organelles.

- Eukaryotes: contain membrane‑enclosed compartments (organelles), including nucleus.

Categories:

1. Bacteria (prokaryote)

2. Animal, Plant, Fungi, Protists (eukaryotes)

## Prokaryote Structure

- Capsule

- Cytoplasm

- Ribosomes (the only organelle in prokaryotes)

- Nucleoid (circular DNA region, no nuclear envelope)

- Cell membrane (phospholipid bilayer)

- Cell wall: composed of peptidoglycan (peptide + sugar)

## Bacteria special structures

- Flagella: for movement

- Pili: for attachment

Eukaryotes: Membrane‑bound Organelles

Organelles are membrane‑enclosed compartments; each carries out specific cellular functions.

List of eukaryotic organelles:

1. Ribosome (non‑membrane‑bound; produce polypeptides)

2. Nucleus (double‑membrane / two lipid bilayers)

3. Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum, Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum

4. Golgi apparatus

5. Lysosomes

6. Vacuoles & vesicles

7. Mitochondria (double membrane)

8. Chloroplasts (double membrane)

9. Peroxisomes

## Cell wall comparison

- Bacteria cell wall: peptidoglycan

- Plant cell wall: cellulose

## Nucleus

- Enclosed by nuclear envelope (double membrane).

- Nucleolus: dense region inside nucleus, produces ribosomes for protein synthesis.

- Nuclear pore: openings on nuclear envelope; allows large RNA‑containing molecules to pass in/out of nucleus.

## Rough ER & Smooth ER

- Rough ER: ribosomes bound on cytoplasmic side; interior lumen space is continuous with nuclear envelope.

- Function: synthesize polypeptide on bound ribosomes; package newly‑made proteins, transport them via vesicles toward Golgi apparatus.

- Smooth ER: connected to Rough ER; no ribosomes attached.

- Function:

1. Synthesize phospholipids, lipids, steroids.

2. Detoxify harmful substances (convert polar toxic molecules into non‑polar so they can exit cell).

ER side note:

- Lumen side: inner space of ER

- Cyto side / cytoplasmic side: faces cytosol

- Bound ribosomes on Rough ER (cytoplasmic side): feed growing polypeptide chain into ER lumen.

- Polypeptide chain naming: N‑terminus (newly added end), C‑terminus (older end).

## Golgi apparatus

- Internal spaces are separated, not continuous with ER.

- Cis face: receiving side, accepts incoming vesicles from ER.

- Trans face: shipping side, bud off new vesicles for transport.

- Function: modify, sort, package proteins; send proteins to different destinations (outside cell or lysosomes).

## Lysosomes

- Spherical membrane‑bound sacs.

- Contain many hydrolytic enzymes.

- Function: break down broken / malfunctioning macromolecules, recycle raw materials.

1. Receive enzyme‑containing vesicles from Golgi apparatus.

2. Digest damaged proteins / organelles; break molecules down to monomers (amino acids etc.) for reuse.

## Peroxisomes

- Spherical membrane‑bound sacs.

- Convert toxic hydrogen peroxide ($\ce{H2O2}$) into water inside organelle.

## Ribosomes

- Two types:

1. Bound ribosome: attached to Rough ER membrane.

2. Free ribosome: suspended in cytoplasm.

- Eukaryote ribosome: 80S (larger)

- Prokaryote (bacteria) ribosome: 70S (smaller)

- General function: synthesize polypeptide chains.

## Mitochondria (double‑membrane organelle)

- Outer membrane: smooth.

- Inner membrane highly folded → cristae; folding increases surface area to carry out more cellular respiration, produce more ATP energy.

- Inner fluid compartment: matrix. Contains 70S ribosomes inside matrix.

- Function: produce ATP via cellular respiration.

## Chloroplast (double‑membrane organelle, plant cells only)

- Inside structure: thylakoids; stacked thylakoids form granum.

- Fluid compartment: stroma.

- Function: site of photosynthesis.

## Vacuole (plant cell large central vacuole)

- Store ions and water.

- Maintain turgor pressure inside plant cell.

## Cytoskeleton

- Helps maintain cell shape, provides structural support for cell.

## Endomembrane System

Components: nuclear envelope, rough ER, smooth ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vacuoles, vesicles, plasma membrane.

Function: synthesize, modify, sort, package and transport proteins & lipids within eukaryotic cell.

Mitochondria and chloroplasts are NOT part of endomembrane system.

## Endosymbiotic Theory

Theory: Mitochondria and chloroplasts evolved from free‑living ancient prokaryotes engulfed by ancestral eukaryotic host cell, became permanent organelles instead of being digested.

- Aerobic bacterium → mitochondrion

- Photosynthetic cyanobacterium → chloroplast

## Four key pieces of evidence

1. Double‑membrane structure: consistent with host cell wrapping membrane around engulfed bacterium.

2. Possess their own circular DNA, separate from nuclear DNA (similar to bacterial DNA).

3. Contain 70S ribosomes, same as prokaryotes (different from eukaryotic cytoplasmic 80S ribosomes).

4. Replicate independently inside host cell by binary fission, the bacterial reproduction method.

## Plasma Membrane (Cell Membrane)

- Main building block: phospholipid bilayer.

- Amphipathic phospholipid: polar hydrophilic head, non‑polar hydrophobic tails.

## Membrane‑associated molecules

1. Integral protein: pass fully through the phospholipid bilayer.

2. Peripheral protein: attached to membrane surface (cytoplasmic side or outer side).

3. Cholesterol: modulates membrane fluidity and permeability. At low temperature, cholesterol reduces membrane fluidity.

4. Glycoprotein: protein + carbohydrate chain on extracellular surface.

Membrane permeability rule: Small non‑polar substances can pass through membrane directly without protein assistance.

## Passive Transport (no energy required; moves down concentration gradient)

## Diffusion

- Random thermal movement of molecules toward equilibrium. Molecules move from higher concentration → lower concentration.

- Factors increasing diffusion rate:

1. Smaller molecule size

2. Higher temperature

3. Larger concentration gradient

## Facilitated Diffusion

- Passive transport for polar / charged molecules that cannot cross bilayer directly.

- Move down concentration gradient; assisted by channel proteins or carrier proteins.

- Transport proteins are highly specific to substrate.

## Osmosis

- Net movement of water across semi‑permeable membrane. Water moves from hypotonic side → hypertonic side.

- Isotonic environment: solute concentration equal on both sides; water in‑flow rate equals out‑flow rate, net water movement = zero.

- Turgor pressure: internal pressure pushing plant cell membrane against cell wall; prevents excess water from entering plant cell.

## Water Potential

Higher solute concentration → solute potential more negative (farther from zero).

- Water moves from higher water potential → lower water potential.

## Active Transport (requires energy input)

- Molecules move against concentration gradient. Concentration gradient magnitude does not affect active‑transport speed.

- Energy source 1: ATP hydrolysis ATP—ADP+Pi).

## Sodium‑Potassium Pump

- Carrier protein performing active transport.

- Pumps 3 Na⁺ out of cell; 2 K⁺ into cell. Creates more negative environment inside cell, more positive outside.

- Protein undergoes conformational shape change during transport cycle powered by ATP hydrolysis.

- Another Energy source: ion gradient (co‑transport). Energy released as one ion flows down its concentration gradient drives another molecule against its gradient.

## Bulk Transport (require energy)

1. Endocytosis: bring large macromolecules into eukaryotic cell; may depend on receptor proteins on membrane.

2. Exocytosis: expel large macromolecules / particles out of eukaryotic cell.
